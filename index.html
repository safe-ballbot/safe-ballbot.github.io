Safe-Ballbot: Tilt-Consistent MPC with Control Barrier Functions for Ballbot Navigation

Anonymous Authors

Paper (soon) Code (soon)
TL;DR

A ballbot must lean to accelerate. We plan on a simple jerk model with control barrier functions, bound its acceleration by the steepest lean that a tilt-safe tracker (HSMC-ESO-ECBF) allows, and inflate predicted obstacles with conformal prediction. The result avoids static, moving, rotating, concave and unpredictable obstacles in MuJoCo, plans in about 10 ms, and never asks the robot to lean more than it safely can.

Jerk-model MPC-CBF

A simple planning model with control barrier functions for collision avoidance.

Tilt-consistent bound

Acceleration is limited by the steepest lean the tracker can safely reach.

HSMC-ESO-ECBF tracker

Tilt-safe tracking that keeps the lean angle below its limit.

Conformal prediction

Predicted obstacles are inflated to cover unpredictable motion.

MuJoCo simulation

All experiments run on a high-fidelity, contact-rich MuJoCo model of the ballbot rather than on the simplified models used for design. The controllers and the planner never see this model; they only act on it.

The robot model is adapted from the open-source asRoBallet MuJoCo model.

  • Free-floating body balancing on a ball, driven by three omni wheels spaced 120° apart with axles tilted 45°.
  • Every passive roller of the omni wheels is simulated, with its own contacts against the ball.
  • Ball–floor contact with sliding, torsional and rolling friction on an elliptic friction cone.
  • Coulomb friction and damping in the wheel and roller joints, absent from the design models.
  • Wheel torques saturated at ±4 N·m; controllers run at the simulation rate.
